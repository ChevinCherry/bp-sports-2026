#### 1. Steal the Bacon
Put teams on either side of the tarp. Line them up smallest to largest and assign them numbers down the line (smallest being 1). Place a watermelon covered in Vaseline in the middle, and then call numbers. The called number from each team then rushes to the middle and tries to bring the watermelon back to their side. First team to do so wins.

#### 4. Long Shot
Players take on one of 3 roles: Launcher, Holder, or Target. Each team sets up 3 hoola hoops, 2 slingshots and 2 buckets of water balloons on opposite sides of the field. Targets stand in the hoola hoops and try not to get hit (without leaving the hoop). Holders hold each slingshot in place for the launchers. Launchers run between the buckets and the slingshots, grabbing water balloons, and shooting them at the opposing team's targets. Team who eliminates all the other team's Target's first wins. Once a Target is out, they become a Launcher.

#### 5. Water Balloon Volleyball
Teams set up on either side of the net, each member pulling the outer edge of a bedsheet to make a platform. One team starts with a water balloon sitting on their sheet, and uses the sheet to launch it over the net. To score a point, you must pop the water balloon on the other teams side, either in their sheet or on their court. Team with the most points at the end wins.

#### 6. Rest
OFFICIAL RULES OF REST.  
A Regulation Non-Sport for Sanctioned Competitive Relaxation

Article I — Objective of the Game

1.1. The objective of Rest. is to rest.  
1.2. There is no secondary objective.  
1.3. Any team found pursuing a secondary objective will be assumed to be playing a different game and disqualified from this one.  
1.4. Victory is not achieved. Victory is received, passively, at the end of the round, by whoever rested the most correctly.  

Article II — Team Composition

2.1. Teams may consist of 1 to infinity players, though infinity is discouraged for space reasons.  
2.2. There is no minimum energy requirement to participate. In fact, energy is disqualifying (see Article IV).  
2.3. A team captain must be appointed to be responsible for absolutely nothing, as there is nothing to manage.  

Article III — The Playing Field

3.1. The field of play is any patch of shade, grass, or minimally damp ground designated by camp staff.  
3.2. Boundaries are optional and unenforced, since leaving the field to go get a freezie faster is, paradoxically, against the spirit of resting.  
3.3. Field markings, if present, should be ignored, as looking at them requires effort.  

Article IV — Core Rule of Play

4.1. Each team member must rest.  
4.2. Resting is defined as: sitting, lying down, reclining, or otherwise assuming a posture inconsistent with productivity.  
4.3. Standing is permitted but frowned upon by the judges' panel (see Article IX) as insufficiently restful.  
4.4. Running, jogging, or any cardiovascular activity is strictly prohibited and will result in immediate freezie forfeiture.  
4.5. Sudden movements of any kind — jumping, cheering, high-fiving — are considered "athletic contamination" and penalized.  

Article V — The Freezie Provision

5.1. Freezies will be distributed to all participating teams as standard equipment for the sport of Resting.  
5.2. Freezies are not prizes. Freezies are uniforms. You are expected to be holding, consuming, or otherwise coexisting with a freezie at all times.  
5.3. Dropping your freezie is a minor infraction (see Article X).  
5.4. Finishing your freezie too quickly, in a manner suggesting enthusiasm or haste, may be flagged by judges as "trying too hard."  
5.5. Sharing freezies is allowed, and even commended, as an act of teamwork that requires no physical exertion whatsoever.  

Article VI — Permitted Activities While Resting

6.1. Lying flat: permitted.  
6.2. Staring at clouds: permitted, encouraged.  
6.3. Light conversation: permitted, provided volume remains under "excited camper" decibel levels.  
6.4. Napping: permitted, and arguably the highest form of gameplay.  
6.5. Humming: permitted, singing: also permitted, but full musical numbers require prior approval from a counselor to avoid disqualifying the whole team for "putting on a show," which is effort.  

Article VII — Prohibited Activities While Resting

7.1. Push-ups.  
7.2. Sit-ups.  
7.3. Cartwheels.  
7.4. Any form of tag, spontaneously initiated.  
7.5. Discussing strategy for a future, more athletic game. This is considered "pre-effort" and is banned under the spirit of the sport.  
7.6. Getting up "just to stretch." Stretching is suspicious. Stay down.  

Article VIII — Scoring

8.1. Points are awarded for Restfulness, a metric determined entirely by vibes.  
8.2. A team lying down motionless for the full duration receives maximum Restfulness.  
8.3. A team caught sitting upright, alert, and "ready for the next activity" receives reduced Restfulness, as this indicates insufficient commitment to the bit.  
8.4. Snoring is worth bonus points, at judges' discretion, for Commitment to the Craft.  

Article IX — Officiating

9.1. All counselors present are automatically judges.  
9.2. Judges are not required to move, evaluate actively, or pay close attention, as this would violate the spirit of the event they are overseeing.  
9.3. Disputes over scoring will be settled by whichever judge is closest to a shady spot.  

Article X — Penalties

10.1. Minor Infraction (dropped freezie, mild fidgeting): warning.  
10.2. Major Infraction (standing up suddenly, jogging in place, attempting jumping jacks): loss of one (1) Restfulness point.  
10.3. Severe Infraction (starting an impromptu game of tag, doing burpees, generally being athletic): full disqualification and forced participation in the next, more active event.  

Article XI — Tiebreakers

11.1. In the event of a tie, both teams shall rest for an additional two (2) minutes.  
11.2. If still tied, the team with the more finished freezie wins.  
11.3. If still tied after that, everyone wins, because insisting on a definitive winner is, itself, a form of effort.  

Article XII — Conduct and Sportsmanship

12.1. Excessive competitiveness about who is resting "correctly" is, ironically, poor sportsmanship and un-restful.  
12.2. Trash talk is permitted only in a low, drowsy murmur.  
12.3. Teams are encouraged to congratulate each other on a well-rested game, ideally without getting up to do so.  

Article XIII — Miscellaneous

13.1. Weather does not affect gameplay, since the correct response to any weather is to keep resting.  
13.2. In case of rain, teams may relocate to a covered area, slowly.  
13.3. This rulebook may be amended at any time by camp staff, though doing so requires effort and is therefore discouraged.  

END OF OFFICIAL RULES.  
Please rest responsibly.

#### 1. Gaga Ball
The goal of the game is to be the last player standing. All players start in the ring. A ball is dropped in and the game begins. You can only hit the ball with a closed fist and you must yell "GAGA" every time you hit the ball. If the ball hits you anywhere but your fist, you are out and must leave the ring. Last man standing wins.

#### 2. Anything but Football
The game is played in rounds, setup the same way as Catch the Bacon (smallest the largest, round starts by calling numbers to enter the field). However, the rules change each round. Unless otherwise specified, all normal football/soccer rules apply (score in opponents net, no hands). There are no goalies. Instead of team throwins, just toss the balls to the team who should have possession. Rounds last 2-3 minutes, or until 1 team scores. Here are some example rounds, but feel free to make up anything crazy you want:

1. Multi-ball: 3 balls are in play, whoever scores 2/3, or has scored more at the end of the round wins.
2. American: Use an American Football instead of a Soccer Ball
3. Exercise Ball: Use an Exercise Ball instead of a Soccer Ball, and no goalie allowed
4. Blindfolded Counsellors: Counselors play a 2v2 blindfolded, their campers shout instructions from the sideline about where to go
5. Hand Ball: Use your hands instead of your feet (no holding the ball)
6. No Hand No Foot Ball: Use anything but your hands or your feet

#### 3. Tchoukball
Two teams each defend a frame, positioned at each end of the court. To score, a team throws the ball at either frame so it bounces off and hits the ground before the other team can catch it, and the ball must land outside the defending team's marked semi-circle to count. If a defender catches the rebound, or the ball lands inside the semi-circle or out of bounds, no point is scored and possession switches to the other team. Players may take up to 3 steps while holding the ball and hold it for up to 3 seconds before passing or shooting. No contact, tackling, or blocking is allowed.
